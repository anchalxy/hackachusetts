BOTTOM LINE
614 residents reported rodent activity through BOS:311 and checked back after 7 days. The median time to a first city response was 31 hrs. 34% of tickets were closed with no visible action, and 13% were still open with no change after a week. East Boston had the slowest median response (47 hrs, n = 35); Roslindale had the fastest (17 hrs).

KEY FINDINGS
1. 14% of reports waited more than 3 days for a first response.
2. After 7 days: 29% baited, 24% inspected, 34% closed with no visible action, 13% still open.
3. Median response in East Boston was 2.8x the median in Roslindale.
4. Most common sign reported: overflowing or open trash (39%).

ABOUT THIS DATA
Residents were asked to: Find a sign of rodents in public space, such as burrows, droppings, or overflowing or open trash; Report it through BOS:311 and enter the ticket number in the app; Take one photo; After 7 days, record what happened: no change, inspected, baited, or closed.
Opt-in sample, not a random survey. Residents 18–34 are 60% of responses and residents 65+ are 9%; Allston residents are 40%. Figures describe participants, not all residents.
Aggregated and pseudonymized. No names, emails, addresses or individual identities are included. Quotes are attributed by neighborhood, and by age group only when that group has 10 or more participants.
