TOBoston Public Works, Cummins Highway project team
FROMTea Party resident input program
RECummins Highway Community Walkthrough, Sat, Oct 24, 2026, 11 a.m.–12:30 p.m., starting at Cummins Highway and Fairway Street (agenda)
BOTTOM LINE
262 residents audited the redesigned Cummins Highway. Lighting was the lowest-rated measure overall (2.7 out of 5). Wheelchair and mobility-aid users gave the lowest score of any group: 2.0 for bus stop access, versus 3.5 for people walking. The walk signal at Itasca St averaged 15.1 seconds, the shortest of the 5 crossings timed.
KEY FINDINGS
  1. Lighting averaged 2.7/5 across all 262 audits, the lowest of the four measures.
  2. Wheelchair and mobility-aid users (n = 42) rated bus stop access 1.5 points lower than people walking.
  3. Itasca St averaged 15.1s of walk signal over 125 timings, 12.0s less than Fairway St.
  4. Car parked on sidewalk or crosswalk was the most-reported obstruction (25% of audits). Among wheelchair and stroller users the top problem was missing or broken curb ramp (47%).

ABOUT THIS DATA

Residents were asked to: Choose a mode: walking, stroller, wheelchair or mobility aid, or bike; Start at Fairway Street and cover at least 3 intersections of the redesigned stretch; At each crossing, time the walk signal with the in-app timer and note anything blocking the path; Rate four things from 1 to 5: sidewalk condition, crossing safety, bus stop access, and lighting; Take at least 2 photos (one of the best spot, one of the worst).

Opt-in sample, not a random survey. Residents 18–34 are 64% of responses and residents 65+ are 6%; Mattapan residents are 42%. Figures describe participants, not all residents.

Aggregated and pseudonymized. No names, emails, addresses or individual identities are included. Quotes are attributed by neighborhood, and by age group only when that group has 10 or more participants.
