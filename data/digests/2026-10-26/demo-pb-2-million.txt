BOTTOM LINE
980 residents built their own $2 million ballot from the seven priced projects. Neighborhood Fresh Food Access was funded most often (85% of ballots), followed by Immigrant Legal Defense Fund (85%). Community Litter Reduction was ranked last most often (24%). Residents 18–24 and 65+ split most on Community Litter Reduction: 42% vs. 87%.

KEY FINDINGS
1. Funded most often: Neighborhood Fresh Food Access (85%), Immigrant Legal Defense Fund (85%), Workforce Training in the Trades (73%).
2. Ranked #1 most often: Neighborhood Fresh Food Access (26% of ballots).
3. Ranked last most often: Community Litter Reduction (24%).
4. Largest age gap: 42% of residents 18–24 funded Community Litter Reduction, vs. 87% of residents 65+.

ABOUT THIS DATA
Residents were asked to: Pick projects from the nine topic areas the city's forums used: Arts and Culture; Health and Wellbeing; Environment, Sustainability and Food Access; Education, Youth and Families; Public Safety; Facilities, Parks and Recreation; Community Resources; Local Economy and Worker Empowerment; Streets and Sidewalks. Then write one sentence on why your top pick matters.
Opt-in sample, not a random survey. Residents 18–34 are 60% of responses and residents 65+ are 7%; Allston residents are 41%. Figures describe participants, not all residents.
Aggregated and pseudonymized. No names, emails, addresses or individual identities are included. Quotes are attributed by neighborhood, and by age group only when that group has 10 or more participants.
